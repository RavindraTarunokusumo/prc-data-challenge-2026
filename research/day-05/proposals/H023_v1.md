# Hypothesis H023 v1: an equal-weight blend of the routed LightGBM and the routed CatBoost (promotion candidate)

Batch X-D05-S04-0001 (see `H021_v1.md`). **Role:** the Day 5 promotion candidate, judged against E019 (laptop instance E027, `LAPTOP_REFS_v1`). Provenance as H021.

## Research Question

Do two boosted learners that use the same keys differently have errors that are imperfectly correlated? If so, does a fixed equal-weight average beat the champion while keeping the D3-C2 treatment?
- LightGBM uses native categorical splits on collapsed levels, with leaf-wise trees.
- CatBoost uses categorical statistics on raw levels, with symmetric trees.

## Observation

- **E023 (H018 v2) treats D3-C2** (> 3 h band 81 → 5; all rows −2.04 s against E019). It failed promotion only through criterion 2: S1 TIE (+0.20 s), decided by one LIRF NM-present row, 192622644 (D4-C9).
- **The D3-C2 treatment is a forward-risk fix.** January 2026 holds 435 non-LIRF NM-missing rows over 3 h, against a 2025 monthly maximum of 222 (D3-C3). The champion still carries D3-C2 and D3-C3 into any submission.
- **The learning curves** (E027) show LightGBM's validation RMSE flat from about 500 iterations. More rounds of the same learner have little left to give. A second learner is a different source of variance.
- **The single-row S1 constraint, on the laptop** (instances, predictions in s; y 87,002 s on row 192622644):

| Row | y | E027 (E019) | E029 (E023) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 192622644 (S1, LIRF NM-present) | 87,002 | 8,136 | 7,041 |
| 183910286 (W1, LIRF NM-present) | 13,865 | 1,979 | 7,938 |

  - Each 1,000 s by which a candidate lowers its prediction on row 192622644, below E027's, costs about 0.66 s of S1 all-rows RMSE.
  - That is from ΔSSE / (2 · RMSE · n), with S1 RMSE 632.1 s and n = 190,713, near E027's prediction.

## Mechanism

- **Averaging two predictors reduces error variance by their decorrelated part.** For error vectors with RMSE r₁ and r₂ and correlation ρ, the 50/50 blend's MSE is ¼r₁² + ¼r₂² + ½ρr₁r₂. It is below the better component only when ρ is low enough relative to the gap between r₁ and r₂.
- **The two learners differ in three ways** that should decorrelate their errors on normal taxis:
  - tree shape (leaf-wise 255 leaves against symmetric depth 8);
  - categorical representation (gradient-sorted splits on collapsed levels against CTRs on raw levels and combinations);
  - row and split randomness (deterministic against Bayesian bootstrap and random strength).
- **On the tail they will not decorrelate.** Both miss the same day-scale records, so the gain must come from bulk rows.
- **Routing and the D3-C2 treatment are held in both components.** The routed rows take the same ridge in both, so the blend's routed rows are the ridge's prediction.
