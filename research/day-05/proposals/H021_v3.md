## Research Question

At fixed capacity and on the same inputs, do CatBoost's categorical statistics (border share, frequency and target mean, including combinations) over **raw** high-cardinality keys reduce RMSE on normal taxis?

## Observation

- **High-cardinality keys** are the inputs that carried Day 2's static signal: FS1 keys gave −9.1 s on NM-present rows (Day 2 headline). Raw levels are 1.4–4.4 times more numerous than FS2's collapsed levels (table above).
- **Day 4's explicit priors** (H019, E024) added only −0.29 s beyond LightGBM's native categorical handling. That leaves open whether a *different* use of the same keys (categorical statistics and combinations inside the learner) adds signal. It also leaves open whether LightGBM "already holds the keys", which is untested (D4-C7).
- **GPU calibration** (permuted target, R3, no metric; `gpu_calibration.json`):

## Mechanism

- A categorical statistic turns each raw level (a stand, an operator, a destination) into numbers that order levels by target behaviour. A symmetric tree can then split many levels at once by those numbers.
- With integer codes (H022), levels are ordered alphabetically. A split groups levels that merely share a name prefix, so the tree needs many splits to isolate a useful set of levels.
- At fixed depth, iterations and learning rate, the statistics should therefore give H021 a lower error on normal taxis than H022, where stand, operator and destination effects live.
- **Combinations** (stand × runway and similar) let the statistics carry interactions that a depth-8 symmetric tree spends depth on.

## Leakage Analysis

- **CTRs** are computed by CatBoost from the training rows only, over permutations. Validation rows have null targets in the masked view, and their CTR values come from the training statistics.
- **FS2_RAW** uses the same raw keys as FS1, all label P (DATASET_AUDIT §6.2). Removing the collapse uses no target and no validation row.
- **Integer codes (H022)** come from the training vocabulary only, so validation-only levels are NaN.

## Alternative Explanations

- **Clause 1 met:** symmetric depth-8 trees on integer codes already isolate the useful levels (the high-traffic levels dominate), or the statistics are too coarse to help. These cannot be told apart here.
- **Clause 1 not met, but H021 far worse than E029:** the statistics help CatBoost, but the symmetric-tree family is the bottleneck. That is the expected case.
- **Large GPU noise:** clause 1 becomes INCONCLUSIVE by the noise condition.
- **Tail contamination of the target-mean statistic** (if clause 1 is met). The LIRF NM-present convention rows stay in training, with targets up to day scale. One 87,000 s record shifts a 200-row level's mean by about 435 s, and the ordered training-row CTRs of such levels are noisy, so the trees may split on noise. This cannot be told apart from "too coarse" or "the high-traffic levels dominate" here.
