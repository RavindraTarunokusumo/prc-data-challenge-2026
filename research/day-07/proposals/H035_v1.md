### Why now

- The champion E033 (and E019 before it) routes the **LIRF NM-missing subgroup** (LIRF departures without an NM match; label P) to a fold-local E005-class ridge. Day 3 priced that routing (X-D03-S01-0003 (e)): **E019 − E020 = +122.54 s** on the development mean (R1 +161.73, R2 +25.52, R3 +97.23, S1 +211.37, W1 +116.85, S1c +197.52, W1c +46.69). E020 is bit-identical to E019 outside the routed rows, so the whole price sits on the subgroup.
- The routing's premise is true but weak. Most of the subgroup's long "taxi-outs" are a **block-at-schedule recording convention** (Day 1: 83 % of LIRF tail rows; label T, `MVT − SCHED`). The unrouted LightGBM learns the convention; the winsorised ridge cannot.
- **Break-even (Day 3 outcome, cited as required by (e)):** the unrouted fit loses to the ridge on the subgroup only if the convention (tail) rate falls below 0.039 / 0.099 / 0.030 / 0.058 / 0.009 / 0.052 / 0.022 (R1 … W1c). The 2025 rates on those folds are 0.35–0.76.
- (e) made an unrouted candidate admissible from Day 4 on, under four conditions (below). Days 4–6 did not propose one. The researcher found this while preparing the final report and asked the owner, recommending a test; the owner chose to test and set the window (INC-0015).

**Criterion 4: mechanism and its ablation.**
- **Claimed mechanism:** the unrouted model predicts the subgroup's convention tail rows (y ≥ 3,600 s, the Day 1/Day 3 convention proxy). The ridge, fitted on a winsorised target, cannot predict them.
- **Readings**, per development fold (R1, R2, R3, S1, W1), from `compare.py E046 E033`'s rule 7 disclosure (`NM_missing_LIRF`) and `mechanism_check.py E046 E033 LIRF_NM_missing`:

  - (b) all rows outside the subgroup are unchanged (route check: exactly 0.0 s).
- **The ablation is the construction itself:** the candidate differs from E033 only on the subgroup, so (b) isolates the claimed population, and (a) splits it into the claimed tail rows and the rest. No further ablation run is proposed.

- **Criterion 8 reference.** H009 v3's statistic is "against E005". E033's routed rows are an E005-class ridge fitted inside each fold, so the reading is taken against **E033** (the matched reference under ruling R) and reported against **E028** (E005's laptop instance) beside it. **Rule 8 (convention exposure) and the forward-risk rationale.**
- The subgroup is 52–337 rows per development fold, and 107 (2026-01) and 276 (2026-07) rows in the ranking months.
- **S1 expectation at July's rate:** at S1's 2025 convention rate (0.35), the candidate gains about 213 s on S1. Its gain falls with the 2026 rate and turns into a loss below the S1 break-even 0.058, under a sixth of the lowest 2025 month. The 2026 rate is not estimable: block time is blanked in the ranking months.
- **Forward risk, stated:** if the convention rate in a ranking month is r, the candidate's expected subgroup SSE change scales roughly as (gain on tail rows × r/r₂₀₂₅) + (loss on bulk rows × (1 − r)/(1 − r₂₀₂₅)). The candidate loses to E033 in that month only for r below the fold-wise break-even (0.009–0.099). **This is a bet on the convention persisting**, pre-registered as such. Its 2025 evidence is that the rate is 0.35–0.76 in every month that has a fold. December (H) is the only out-of-sample month left.
- **Rule 12:** subgroup predictions above 3,600 s are counted by fold. They are expected in roughly the subgroup's tail share, which is the mechanism.

**Rule 6 (row concentration).** The gain lives on 52–337 rows per fold, so the top-10 row share of the SSE change will be large; the report shows it per fold. **Rule 7** is unchanged except the `NM_missing_LIRF` cell, which carries the whole change. **Rule 11:** every subgroup figure is reported next to the all-rows figure. **Rule 13:** the candidate adds no stochastic component; its evaluated figures are E033's draw (D5-C9) on non-subgroup rows. **Rule 14:** the point contrast against each E033 draw (E033, E034, E041) is reported beside the governing reading against E033.
