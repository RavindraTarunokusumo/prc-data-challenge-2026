## The candidate

**H038** keeps E046's predictions on every row outside the subgroup (`blending.override_fitted`, base E046). On the subgroup (LIRF, `flt_missing == 1`; `routed.route_mask`; label P) it predicts the conditional mean of a two-component population (`prc.models.mixture.convention_mixture`):

```text
pred = p · max(d_sched, 0) + (1 − p) · g        (g alone where d_sched is null)
```

- **The convention label** is c = |y − `d_sched`| < 120 s. This is Day 1's definition (PHASE_CLOSE_D01 review (c)), used unchanged. It is computed on training rows only.
- **p:** P(c = 1 | x). A LightGBM binary classifier on the fold's training rows of the subgroup, with FS2 features:
  - learning rate 0.05, 15 leaves, `min_data_in_leaf` 20, 200 rounds;
  - no subsampling; 4 threads.
- **The convention component:** `d_sched` itself, floored at 0 s. Nothing is fitted. Under the convention, the recorded off-block time is SCHED_TIME, so y = MVT − SCHED = `d_sched`.
- **g, the normal-taxi component:** a LightGBM regression on the fold's LIRF training rows with c = 0, with FS2 features. It uses E045's parameters: learning rate 0.05, 255 leaves, `min_data_in_leaf` 100, 1,000 rounds.
- **The parameters were fixed before any data was looked at.** They are in `research/day-08/proposals/H038_params.yaml`, committed at `9f653a9` before the design pilot ran. Nothing was tuned.
- **Why the structure should help (Gneiting 2011).** Under squared error, the best point forecast is the conditional mean.
  - A tree regression trained on all rows represents `d_sched` only through bounded leaf values. So a single LightGBM cannot carry the convention component's exact identity y = `d_sched` across a range of up to 1–36 hours.
  - The mixture carries it exactly. It leaves only the mixing weight and the normal component to be learned.

**Rule 10:** a new configuration. No earlier experiment fits a classifier or uses `d_sched` as a prediction component.

## Literature (general methodology only)

- Gneiting, T. (2011), "Making and evaluating point forecasts", JASA 106: under squared error the optimal point forecast is the conditional mean.
- McLachlan, G. and Peel, D. (2000), *Finite Mixture Models*, Wiley, ch. 1: the mean of a two-component mixture is the probability-weighted mean of its components, so the point forecast needs a calibrated mixing probability and each component's conditional mean.
- Hastie, Tibshirani, Friedman (2009), §9.2: tree ensembles represent a function by bounded piecewise-constant leaves and do not extrapolate an identity such as y = `d_sched`.
