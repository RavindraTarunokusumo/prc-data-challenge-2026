## Research Question

How much of the congestion effect is available **at the off-block proxy (P)**, and how much needs the row's own taxi interval (T)? The P part is the congestion contribution available to the causal-only variant that Days 5–7 must report (DATASET_AUDIT §6.2).

## Observation

EDA (H015 v2 Observation; P figures computed with the v1 implementation, which differs on 0.086 % of rows):
- **Residual after the airport median.** The P features reduce it by 0.9–9.3 s each; the largest is `cg_dep_taxiing` at 9.3 s.
- **Residual after the anchor.** They reduce it by only 0.3–1.7 s. The T features reduce it by 9.7–11.8 s. That after-anchor figure partly re-measures the anchor (H015 v2).

## Mechanism

- The state at pushback (the queue, taxiing arrivals and recent throughput) sets the expected waiting time.
- Most of it is already reflected in E017's anchor delta `d_aobt3`, which is itself label T.
- **Expectation.** The P part adds little beyond E017; the T part adds most of C.

## Leakage Analysis

**Inputs.** FS1 plus the 10 P congestion features (H015 v2 table).
- **The added inputs are P (v2).** Each is a count over rows j ≠ i with the row's own contribution removed exactly, so none depends on the row's own takeoff time. This is tested (`test_p_features_invariant_to_own_takeoff`) and verified on real data (`fs2_v2_checks.json`).
- **H017 is still not itself the causal-only variant.** FS1's own T inputs remain (`hour_utc`, `weekday`, the `d_*` deltas). H017 isolates the congestion block's P part: what the causal-only variant gains from congestion, not the causal-only model itself.
