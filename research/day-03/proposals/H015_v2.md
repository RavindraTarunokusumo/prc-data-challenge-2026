## Mechanism

**C (congestion).** Unchanged from v1.
- Taxi-out time is service time plus waiting time. The waiting time is set by the departure queue and by crossing arrival traffic, and the literature models it as a queue at the runway (Idris et al.; Pujet, Delcaire & Feron; Simaiakis & Balakrishnan).
- The queue is reconstructed from admissible movement records:
  - **P state at the off-block proxy:** aircraft taxiing out and in, recent takeoffs, pushbacks and landings, and scheduled demand in the next 30 min;
  - **T state during the row's own taxi:** takeoffs from the airport and the runway, landings, the runway's previous-takeoff gap, and runway throughput in the 15 min before the row's takeoff.
- The T counts measure how many aircraft were served ahead of the row, and how many arrivals competed for the runway, while it taxied.

**R (routing).**
- Rows of the LIRF NM-missing subgroup (the routing key is label P) take the prediction of **E005's model class**: H004's FS0 ridge (α 1.0, winsor 0.005–0.995), fitted on the same fold inside the same run.
- The candidate's predictions on that subgroup are therefore **identical to the champion's**. On the routed subgroup, its exposure to the convention in any month and at any rate is the champion's: zero relative risk, by construction. (Residual exposure elsewhere: see Experimental Isolation.)
- **This is a structural choice, not a threshold** (ruling B). It is pre-registered before any Day 3 run.
- **Rationale, restated on the corrected figures.** The ranking months' subgroup counts are *not* unprecedented: both lie inside 2025's range. The rationale is the **rate**, not the count:
  - the subgroup's convention rate varied by a factor of 2.4 across 2025 months (0.35–0.83) with no target-free predictor, so its 2026 rate is not estimable;
  - the subgroup is 1–2 % of LIRF DEP rows in the ranking months (107 and 276 rows), yet in Day 2 it decided criterion 8 and S1's accuracy;
  - any learned model of the convention is therefore a bet whose 2026 payoff cannot be checked before submission. The champion's model makes no such bet, and routing adopts its behaviour there.
- **It does not rest on E006–E018 outcomes.** The expected magnitudes below cite them only as sizes.

**The P labels now hold (Revision 4, re-implementation).**
- **v1 defect.** v1 removed the self term by subtracting a constant 1. That is correct only when the row counts itself, which depends on its own takeoff. On rows taking off at or before their proxy, five P features carried bits of the row's own takeoff time.
- **v2 fix.** Each count subtracts the row's own contribution **exactly** (`congestion.py`, commit `63923e2`): `1{t_to_i > t_off_i}` for the taxiing counts, and `1{t_to_i ∈ [t_off_i − w, t_off_i)}` for the recent-takeoff counts. The result is a count over rows j ≠ i and does not depend on *t_to_i*. The docstring now says so.
- **Test of the stated property:** `test_p_features_invariant_to_own_takeoff` moves the subject's own takeoff across nine positions, before, at and after its proxy, and asserts every P feature is unchanged. It and `test_p_counts_exclude_self_when_takeoff_precedes_proxy` both fail on the v1 code (6 failures) and pass on v2.
- **Real data** (`fs2_v2_checks.json`):
  - the fix changes exactly the five named features, and no other;
  - it changes them only on rows that take off at or before their proxy: 1,645 changed rows, all among the 1,651 such rows in Jan–Nov 2025;
  - masking invariance still holds on all 1,919,370 rows.
- **Other P features:**
  - "not yet airborne at my *t_off*" uses others' takeoff times only through t_to_j > t_off_i, which is known at *t_off*;
  - the runway is P by assumption (§6.2);
  - for the 1.1 % of rows without `AOBT_3`, the P features are anchored at `EOBT_1` or `SCHED`, the same proxy order that §6.2 uses.

**Inputs.**
- The inputs are DEP takeoff times, runways, `AOBT_3`, `EOBT_1` and `SCHED` of rows of the same view, and ARR landing, in-block and scheduled times.
- **Another DEP row's block time or target is never used.** This is verified on real data and by a unit test.
- All the inputs are in the §6.2 admissible set for a ranking row, and all are present in validation and ranking alike.

**Cross-month.**
- Windows reach only months in the fold's view, and are at most 30 min long.
- SUBMIT_JAN sees 31 Dec 2025. SUBMIT_JUL has no June 2026 context for the first 30 min of 1 July (0.008 % of rows).
- S1's validation month ends before the embargoed August.

**Staleness (§6.5).** The congestion features use no target statistic. The only staleness is the model's: 2025 training for July 2026.

**Routing and fits.** The routing key is P. Both fits use only the fold's training rows.

## Alternative Explanations

1. **The T counts partly re-encode `d_aobt3`,** since the count of takeoffs in (*t_off*, *t_to*) grows with the interval length. E017 already contains `d_aobt3`, so C measures what is added beyond it. H017 bounds how much comes from the state at pushback.
2. **C could come from the NM-missing rows at the other nine airports.** The clause population excludes them.
3. **C could be a perturbation artefact.** The −6.0 s floor is about three times the largest dev-fold perturbation on this population.
4. **The routing could look good or bad on 2025 folds for reasons unrelated to 2026.** That is why R is not claimed as an accuracy mechanism.
